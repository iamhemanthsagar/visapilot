# VisaPilot — EB-1A Master Requirements

**Status:** Domain specification — Phase 1  
**Pathway:** EB-1A / E11 — Alien of Extraordinary Ability  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27  
**Product role:** Evidence-grounded profile intelligence and assessment support; not legal advice.

> This document defines the product's representation of the EB-1A legal structure. It is not a substitute for the INA, 8 CFR, USCIS Policy Manual, binding precedent, or individualized legal advice. Where the legal record is uncertain, VisaPilot must preserve uncertainty and route the issue to professional/legal review rather than inventing a conclusion.

## 1. Regulatory foundation

VisaPilot's EB-1A model is grounded primarily in:

- INA §203(b)(1)(A), 8 U.S.C. §1153(b)(1)(A)
- 8 CFR §204.5(h)
- Relevant USCIS Policy Manual guidance and policy alerts
- Applicable binding precedent
- AAO non-precedent decisions only as illustrative, not controlling law

The statutory framework describes an EB-1A beneficiary as a person with extraordinary ability demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim and achievements recognized in the field through extensive documentation; the person must seek to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability and prospective entry must substantially benefit the United States.

## 2. Overall product model

VisaPilot must not represent EB-1A as a single numeric eligibility score.

The assessment is a structured record containing:

1. Candidate/profile claims and facts
2. Potential criterion matches
3. Criterion propositions
4. Supporting evidence and provenance
5. Criterion-level evidence states
6. Comparable-evidence analysis where relevant
7. Recognition/acclaim timeline and signals
8. Final-merits / totality synthesis
9. Continue-work assessment
10. Prospective-U.S.-benefit assessment
11. Gaps, unresolved questions, and evidence-build actions
12. Professional/legal review flags

The central relationship is:

`CLAIM → PROPOSITION → EVIDENCE → REGULATORY REQUIREMENT → ASSESSMENT STATE`

## 3. EB-1A assessment structure

### Layer A — Profile intelligence

Extract candidate claims/facts from the CV and supplied documents. Determine which regulatory criteria a claim could potentially relate to. Do not treat an unverified CV assertion as established evidence.

### Layer B — Initial evidence

Evaluate the candidate against the regulatory initial-evidence structure:

- one-time major internationally recognized award route; or
- at least three of the ten regulatory criteria.

The ten criteria are C1–C10 below.

Meeting three criteria is not represented as an automatic EB-1A conclusion. The record proceeds to the overall/final-merits assessment.

### Layer C — Comparable evidence

If a listed standard does not readily apply to the beneficiary's occupation, comparable evidence may be considered under 8 CFR §204.5(h)(4). Comparable evidence is not a generic fallback for failing a criterion. VisaPilot must separately capture:

- why the listed standard may not readily apply to the occupation;
- the proposed alternative evidence;
- why the alternative is asserted to be comparable to the relevant standard; and
- uncertainty requiring professional/legal review.

### Layer D — Final merits / totality

Assess the record as a whole for the overall EB-1A standard, including sustained acclaim, recognition of achievements in the field, and whether the evidence establishes expertise indicating that the person is among the small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field.

The final-merits layer may consider relevant evidence that does not itself satisfy a particular enumerated criterion. Such evidence must not be silently relabeled as C1–C10 evidence.

### Layer E — Continued work

EB-1A does not require a U.S. job offer or labor certification, but the petition must contain clear evidence that the beneficiary is coming to the United States to continue work in the area of expertise. VisaPilot treats this as a separate requirement.

### Layer F — Prospective U.S. benefit

VisaPilot separately represents the statutory prospective-substantial-benefit requirement. It must not be hidden inside C1–C10 or treated as automatically established by criterion count.

## 4. Core EB-1A standard

### 4.1 Extraordinary ability

Regulatory definition: a level of expertise indicating that the individual is one of the small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor.

Product representation:

- Do not create an artificial C11 called “Extraordinary Ability.”
- Treat this as an overarching assessment synthesized from the record.
- Preserve the evidence supporting the synthesis.
- Do not generate a probability of approval or a legal guarantee.

### 4.2 Sustained national or international acclaim

The assessment must preserve the time dimension of recognition.

Relevant signals may include awards, judging, published material, professional recognition, contributions, leadership/critical roles, and other documented recognition. The product may identify patterns such as recurrent, recent, historic, limited, or unclear recognition, but these are product evidence states—not legal conclusions.

### 4.3 Achievements recognized in the field

The product must distinguish:

- an achievement claimed by the candidate;
- evidence that the achievement occurred;
- recognition of that achievement by others; and
- the scope/independence of the recognition.

Employer-only recognition, self-assertion, independent industry recognition, media coverage, professional-body recognition, and other sources must remain distinguishable.

### 4.4 Very top of the field

VisaPilot must not infer “top of field” from a simple criterion count or from a candidate's own superlative language.

The synthesis should consider the total documented record, including the nature, quality, independence, scope, recency, and consistency of recognition and achievements. Unresolved questions must remain visible.

## 5. Initial evidence — C1 through C10

### C1 — Lesser nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(i).

Core propositions:

- Candidate received the award/prize.
- The award is nationally or internationally recognized.
- The award is for excellence in the field.

Important distinctions:

- award exists ≠ candidate received it;
- finalist/nominee ≠ recipient;
- company/team recognition ≠ automatic individual recognition;
- organization reputation ≠ proof that the award itself is nationally/internationally recognized.

Team awards must be represented without automatically rejecting them; current USCIS guidance confirms that team awards can be considered under this criterion where the beneficiary's receipt/participation is established.

### C2 — Membership in associations requiring outstanding achievements

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(ii).

Core propositions:

- Candidate has/had membership in the relevant association.
- The specific membership level is in the field for which classification is sought.
- The membership requires outstanding achievements.
- Those achievements are judged by recognized national or international experts in the relevant discipline/field.

Important distinctions:

- prestige alone is insufficient;
- invitation-only status is not automatically equivalent to the regulatory requirement;
- ordinary dues, education, experience, or certification requirements do not automatically establish outstanding achievement;
- membership level matters;
- past memberships should not automatically be discarded; current USCIS guidance clarifies that past memberships may be considered.

### C3 — Published material about the beneficiary

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(iii).

Core propositions:

- Published material exists.
- The material is about the beneficiary (not merely a passing mention).
- It relates to the beneficiary's work in the field.
- The publication is professional, a major trade publication, or other major media.
- Required publication metadata (including title, date, and author) can be established, with translation where required.

Important distinctions:

- mention/quote ≠ material about the beneficiary;
- article about employer/project ≠ automatically material about beneficiary;
- publication brand name alone ≠ automatic proof of qualifying publication status.

Publication identifiers, publisher URLs, article IDs, DOI/other source locators, and retrieval provenance should be first-class data.

### C4 — Judging the work of others

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(iv).

Core propositions:

- Candidate participated as a judge.
- The activity involved judging/evaluating the work of others.
- The activity is in the same or an allied field.
- Participation actually occurred, individually or on a panel.

Important distinctions:

- invitation ≠ completed judging;
- committee membership ≠ automatically judging;
- ordinary supervision ≠ automatically judging.

Evidence may include completed reviews, reviewer portals, editorial confirmations, organizer confirmations, scoring sheets, or equivalent records.

### C5 — Original contributions of major significance

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(v).

Core propositions:

- A specific contribution is identified.
- The contribution is attributable to the beneficiary.
- The contribution is original.
- The contribution is in the relevant field.
- There is evidence of significance.
- The significance is sufficiently major in the field.

VisaPilot must separately track originality and significance. A useful or successful employer project does not automatically establish a contribution of major significance in the field.

Potential evidence signals include independent adoption/use, citations, licensing/commercialization, objective impact, independent coverage, expert evidence, or other corroboration. These are evidence types, not hardcoded legal thresholds.

Claim-safety flags should identify unsupported superlatives and quantification claims such as “world-leading,” “used by millions,” or “top 1%” when documentary support is absent.

### C6 — Authorship of scholarly articles

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(vi).

Core propositions:

- Candidate is an author/coauthor of the article.
- The article exists and can be identified.
- The article is scholarly.
- It concerns the relevant field.
- It was published in a qualifying professional/major trade publication or other major media.

Important distinctions:

- publication ≠ automatically scholarly article;
- dissertation ≠ automatically qualifying published article;
- general-audience article ≠ automatically scholarly;
- acknowledgments/editorial contribution ≠ automatically authorship.

### C7 — Display of work at artistic exhibitions/showcases

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(vii).

Core propositions:

- Candidate's work is identified.
- The work is in the relevant artistic field.
- The work was actually displayed.
- The display occurred at an artistic exhibition/showcase.

Invitation or selection alone does not establish actual display. Online/hybrid exhibition questions that depend on current legal interpretation should be preserved as uncertainty/counsel-review issues rather than forced into a binary rule without authoritative support.

### C8 — Leading or critical role

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(viii).

Core propositions:

- Candidate performed a leading or critical role.
- The role is attributable to the candidate's actual responsibilities and performance, not merely title.
- The role was for an organization/establishment.
- The organization/establishment has a distinguished reputation.

Important distinctions:

- impressive title ≠ automatic leading/critical role;
- famous organization ≠ automatic distinguished-reputation proof;
- lack of direct reports ≠ automatic failure of the critical-role concept.

The evidence model should capture actual scope, decision authority, responsibility, project/function ownership, team structure, outcomes, and evidence of organizational reputation.

### C9 — High salary or significantly high remuneration

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(ix).

Core propositions:

- Candidate commanded salary/remuneration at the claimed level.
- The remuneration is high/significantly high in relation to others in the field.
- The comparison population/context is meaningful.

VisaPilot must not hardcode a universal salary threshold. Comparative context should preserve occupation, specialization, seniority, geography, period, and compensation type where available.

### C10 — Commercial success in the performing arts

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(3)(x).

Core propositions:

- Candidate participated in a performing-arts work/activity.
- The activity falls within the relevant performing-arts context.
- Commercial success is established.
- The commercial result can be meaningfully attributed to the candidate/work.
- Objective commercial evidence exists or is identified as required.

The regulation expressly identifies box-office receipts and record/cassette/CD/video sales as examples. Modern metrics such as streaming should not be automatically equated with the regulatory examples without appropriate legal review.

## 6. One-time major award route

The initial-evidence regulation provides an alternative to the three-criterion route: evidence of a one-time achievement that is a major, internationally recognized award.

VisaPilot must represent this separately from C1. C1 concerns lesser nationally/internationally recognized prizes or awards; the one-time major-award route is a separate regulatory path.

## 7. Comparable evidence

Comparable evidence is governed by 8 CFR §204.5(h)(4).

VisaPilot must ask:

1. What is the beneficiary's occupation/field?
2. Does the listed standard readily apply to that occupation?
3. If not readily applicable, what is the occupation-specific rationale?
4. What alternative evidence is proposed?
5. Why is the alternative evidence asserted to be comparable to the relevant standard?
6. What evidence supports that rationale?
7. What remains uncertain and should receive professional/legal review?

Comparable evidence is not a generic substitute for weak evidence, a failed criterion, or missing documents.

## 8. Final merits / totality

After the initial-evidence analysis, VisaPilot must synthesize the record rather than simply count criteria.

The final-merits record should preserve:

- sustained-acclaim signals and timeline;
- recognition of achievements in the field;
- nature and significance of achievements;
- independence and quality of recognition;
- scope of impact/recognition;
- consistency and recency;
- corroboration and contradictions;
- relevant evidence that does not fit a particular criterion;
- unresolved issues.

The output is an evidence-grounded synthesis for professional/legal review, not a probability of approval.

## 9. Continue work in area of expertise

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR §204.5(h)(5).

The product must separately assess whether the record contains clear evidence that the beneficiary is coming to the United States to continue work in the area of expertise.

Potential evidence includes:

- prospective employer letters;
- prearranged commitments/contracts; and
- a beneficiary statement describing plans to continue the work.

No U.S. job offer or labor certification is required for EB-1A under this provision.

## 10. Prospective substantial U.S. benefit

Statutory basis: INA §203(b)(1)(A)(iii), 8 U.S.C. §1153(b)(1)(A)(iii).

VisaPilot must represent this as a separate statutory requirement. It must not be inferred solely from the number of criteria satisfied.

The product may organize evidence concerning the proposed U.S. activity and anticipated contribution, but must not promise or predict USCIS acceptance.

## 11. Evidence architecture

Every material assessment should be traceable through:

`Source document → Claim → Proposition → Evidence → Regulatory requirement → Result → Gap/action`

A single evidence item may support multiple propositions and criteria. A proposition may have multiple evidence items.

Evidence status should remain distinct from claim fit.

Example:

- Claim fit: POTENTIAL_MATCH
- Originality: SUPPORTED
- Major significance: INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
- Attribution: SUPPORTED
- Overall C5: COUNSEL_REVIEW

The product should never convert “not yet evidenced” into “false” without a basis for doing so.

## 12. Assessment states

### Claim-fit states

- NOT_RELEVANT
- POTENTIAL_MATCH
- UNCLEAR

### Evidence/proposition states

- SUPPORTED
- PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED
- INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
- UNVERIFIED
- CONFLICTING
- NOT_SUPPORTED
- NOT_APPLICABLE

### Professional/legal review state

- COUNSEL_REVIEW

These states are intentionally descriptive. They are not approval probabilities.

## 13. LLM boundaries

### LLM responsibilities

The LLM may:

- extract claims and facts from unstructured documents;
- normalize natural-language descriptions;
- identify potential criterion matches;
- decompose claims into propositions;
- interpret semantic relationships between claims and evidence;
- identify ambiguity, contradictions, unsupported superlatives, and missing factual links;
- synthesize evidence-grounded patterns for strategy, gaps, and roadmap prose.

### Deterministic responsibilities

The deterministic engine owns:

- the structured regulatory requirements;
- criterion identifiers and proposition definitions;
- source/version metadata;
- legal-state transitions;
- evidence provenance and persistence;
- criterion applicability logic encoded from the specification;
- assessment-state validation;
- final application state;
- report reproducibility.

### LLM must not be authoritative for

- what the regulation says;
- whether an invented legal requirement exists;
- automatic approval/denial probability;
- treating three criteria as automatic EB-1A eligibility;
- inventing evidence;
- converting unsupported candidate assertions into verified facts;
- guaranteeing counsel or USCIS acceptance.

## 14. Product safety / scope boundaries

VisaPilot is an assessment and evidence-intelligence product. It must:

- expose provenance;
- preserve uncertainty;
- distinguish claim, evidence, and legal requirement;
- identify professional/legal review points;
- avoid fabricated citations or evidence;
- avoid presenting heuristic scores as legal probabilities;
- avoid silently substituting general model knowledge for the configured regulatory source layer.
